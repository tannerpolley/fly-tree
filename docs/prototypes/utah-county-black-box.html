Your waters at a glance

RiverMiddle Provo
Below Jordanelle to Deer Creek. Tailwater, stable cold flows, heavy midge / BWO / PMD / drake / sow-bug diet. Rainbows, browns, cutthroat, whitefish. Waders needed.
RiverLower Provo
Provo Canyon below Deer Creek. Bigger, faster, more pocket water and runs; caddis, BWO, stoneflies, streamers for browns. Waders needed.
Small streamThe “Forks”
I assumed American Fork / Spanish Fork / Hobble Creek-type freestone streams. Smaller water, shorter casts, runoff in May–June, terrestrials in summer. Tell me if you meant something else.
Still waterBartholomew Pond, Springville
Spring-fed park pond at the mouth of Hobble Creek Canyon. Regularly stocked rainbows (also cutthroat / tiger), some largemouth bass. Fish from the north shore; no waders needed. Park hours only; seasonal closure around Oct 10 (verify).

Rivers vs still water

River / stream (Provo, Forks)Still water (Bartholomew Pond)
Where fish sitSeams, runs, pocket water, tail-outs, behind rocks, undercut banks. Look for slow water next to fast.Drop-offs, weed edges, inlets, shade, cruising lanes along the shoreline.
PresentationDead drift — fly moves at current speed. Mend line.Static or slow retrieve — fish hunt, so you create movement (strip, hand-twist, pause).
Best fly typesNymphs (≈70–80% of feeding), dry in hatches, streamers for browns.Chironomids, leeches, Woolly Buggers, damsels, egg/pellet (stocked), poppers for bass.
RigIndicator nymph, tight-line, dry-dropper, single dry, streamer.Floating line + long leader + indicator (chironomid), strip streamer, sink-tip / intermediate if deep.
Leader9′ 4X–5X (6X in low clear water).9–12′ 3X–5X; longer = more stealth.
WadingWaders + boots essential (cold, deep, current).Bank fishing; waders optional.
Time of dayWinter: midday warmth. Summer: early / late + hatches. Hatches follow insect timing.Low light (dawn/dusk) best; stocked trout feed most of the day.
Weather effectOvercast/drizzle → BWO and mayfly hatches. Rain raises/clouds water → worms, big dark flies.Wind pushes food to a shore → fish that bank. Sun → fish go deeper.

Tailwater vs freestone (why Provo ≠ the Forks)

Middle Provo (tailwater)
Dam-controlled so temps/flows are steady; fish feed on midges, sow bugs, BWO all year; selective, so small flies and light tippets. Heavy pressure.
Forks (freestone)
Snowmelt-driven: high and stained in May–June (go big & dark, fish slow edges), clear and low by late summer (smaller flies, terrestrials, longer leaders). Fish are less picky.
